TOPIC
=====
The Indian WhatsApp forward: "Protein powder se kidney fail ho jaata hai."
("Protein powder causes kidney failure.")

AUDIENCE
========
Indians who train, or want to, and their families. Specifically the person who
has been talked out of protein by a relative forwarding a health message. Many
are vegetarian and already well below their protein target.

WHY THIS MYTH IS DIFFERENT FROM MOST
====================================
It is half true, and that is exactly why it spreads and why it is hard to
dislodge. Protein restriction is genuine, mainstream medical advice — for
people with existing chronic kidney disease. That advice, written for a sick
population, got generalised into a warning for everybody. The correction is
therefore not "your uncle is wrong" but "your uncle is repeating real advice
that was never written for you."

THE EVIDENCE (what may and may not be claimed)
==============================================
MAY be claimed:
- In people with healthy kidneys, long-term studies of resistance-trained
  adults eating high protein have found no damage to kidney function.
- The 0.8 g/kg figure people cite is a minimum to prevent deficiency, not a
  ceiling or a safety limit.
- People training regularly are generally advised 1.6–2.2 g/kg.
- Whey is a milk derivative. Protein powder is food in powder form, not a drug
  and not a steroid. Paneer, dahi, dal and eggs are the same macronutrient.
- Creatine supplementation raises serum creatinine, a marker used in kidney
  panels, WITHOUT indicating kidney damage. This is a common cause of alarming
  blood reports among people who lift, and it is a measurement artefact rather
  than a disease.

MUST NOT be claimed:
- That protein is safe for everyone. It is not a claim the evidence supports
  and it is not the claim this video makes.
- That anyone should ignore, delay or override medical advice.
- Anything that would read as reassurance to a person who already has reduced
  kidney function.

REQUIRED CAVEAT (non-negotiable, gets its own scene)
====================================================
Anyone with existing kidney disease, diabetes, or high blood pressure should
have their protein intake guided by a doctor. The video must state this
plainly, at full size, not as fine print. A viewer with early kidney disease
must not come away thinking this video was addressed to them.

THE GAP THE CTA POINTS AT
=========================
The practical problem in India is not excess protein — it is deficit. A typical
vegetarian day of poha, dal-rice-sabzi, chai and roti-sabzi lands around
40–50 g of protein. A 70 kg person training four days a week wants roughly
110–150 g. Most people miss the target by about half. The myth makes that gap
wider by scaring people away from the easiest tool for closing it.

APPROVED 10-SCENE SCRIPT (user-confirmed; on-screen text)
=========================================================
1. "Your uncle is right."           sub: Protein does damage kidneys.
2. WhatsApp bubble, "Forwarded many times":
   "Protein powder se kidney fail ho jaata hai 🙏"
3. "That advice is real."           sub: A doctor wrote it.
4. TURN. Prescription slip, patient name lights up:
   "For people who already have kidney disease."   sub: Not for you.
5. "Advice for the sick"  →  "became a warning for everyone."
6. "Healthy kidneys. High protein."  "No damage found."
   sub: long-term studies, trained adults
7. "Kidney disease? Diabetes? High BP?"  "Then ask your doctor."
   sub: This one's not for you either.
8. "Creatine raises creatinine."  "That's a marker. Not damage."
9. "Most Indians don't overshoot protein."  "They miss it by half."
10. CTA. Fitzo — track what you actually eat.
    "Which forward next? 👇"

STRUCTURAL NOTES
================
- The hook agrees with the viewer. That is the pattern interrupt.
- Scene 3 opens a loop ("that advice is real") that scene 4 closes.
- The colour flips at scene 4, the turn.
- Scene 8 is the comment-driver: it explains why a lifter's blood report can
  look alarming when nothing is wrong.
- Scene 7 is the trust beat. A video that admits who it is not for is the one
  people believe.
