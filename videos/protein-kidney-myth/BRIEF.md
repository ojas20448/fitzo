## Intent

Bust the single most damaging fitness myth circulating on Indian WhatsApp:
"protein powder se kidney fail ho jaata hai." The myth stops people hitting a
protein target they are already missing badly, and it spreads because it is
**half true** — protein restriction is real medical advice, just for a
different patient.

The telling is a rug-pull. The reel opens by *agreeing* with the uncle who
forwarded it, then reveals that the advice is a genuine prescription written
for people who already have kidney disease. Agreement is the pattern interrupt;
nobody scrolling expects a myth-bust to start by conceding.

Tone: calm, certain, unsmug. It is not making fun of the person who forwarded
it — it is explaining why they were half right. Confidence comes from
admitting who the video is *not* for.

For Fitzo, an India-first gym app. The CTA is earned by the content: the
correction points at tracking what you actually eat, which is what the app does.

## Customizations

- **The rug-pull structure is the concept** — scene 1 must agree with the myth
  before scene 4 turns it. Do not restructure into a conventional
  claim-then-debunk.
- **Prescription-slip visual world** for the turn (scenes 3–4): the reveal is a
  patient name on a prescription that isn't yours.
- **WhatsApp forward** rendered as the real thing in scene 2 — "Forwarded many
  times" label included. It is the artefact the whole video is about.
- Colour flip at the turn (scene 4) as the retention beat.

## Notes

**This video makes health claims. Two rules override any creative preference:**

1. **Scene 7 — the doctor caveat — is not cuttable and is not shrinkable.**
   Someone with early kidney disease will watch this. The reel must never read
   as permission to ignore their doctor. It also happens to be the most
   trustworthy beat in the piece.

2. **Scene 6's claim is worded "no damage found", never "protein is safe."**
   That is what the evidence supports for *healthy* kidneys. Narrowly right
   beats broadly quotable. Do not upgrade the wording for punchiness.

Other constraints:

- **Plays muted.** Instagram Reels default to sound off, so on-screen text
  carries the entire meaning. Narration is a bonus track, never the sole
  carrier of a claim.
- Max ~6 words per line. Numbers large.
- Do not use a red X / green tick treatment, a talking-head framing, or
  before/after body shots — that is what every other video on this subject
  looks like, and the concept is explicitly built to avoid it.
- The "misses it by half" figure traces to Fitzo's own Learn content: a typical
  Indian vegetarian day lands at 40–50g protein against a 110–150g target for
  a 70kg person training.
- Brand: dark (#000) ground, high-contrast white, Lexend display/body if the
  preset can carry it.
