01 — The uncomfortable finding

Logging has been commoditised

Fitzo just spent a hard session repairing voice logging end-to-end. That work was necessary — it was broken — but it is worth being clear-eyed about what it bought you: parity, not advantage.

In the eighteen months to mid-2026, AI logging went from differentiator to table stakes. Cal AI went from zero to roughly $30M ARR and 15M+ downloads on photo-based logging alone, then was acquired by MyFitnessPal in a deal that closed December 2025. MyFitnessPal now ships voice logging, an AI meal scanner and barcode in the base product. Fitia, MyNetDiary, CalPal, Nutrola and HealthyOne all ship voice-first logging — HealthyOne runs it on gemini-2.5-flash, the same model family Fitzo uses.

Your logging stack — voice, photo (analyzeFoodFromPhoto), barcode, and a 9,923-item region-tagged Indian catalogue on IFCT 2017 — is genuinely good, and better than most on Indian food specifically. But it is a feature every serious competitor now has some version of, funded better than you.

Voice logging is the price of entry to the category. It is not the reason anyone will choose Fitzo over HealthifyMe. Ship it, keep it fast, and stop treating it as the headline.

03 — Positioning

The thing only you have

Fitzo's differentiation is not any single feature. It is an intersection that is structurally hard to assemble, and you have already built it without naming it.

You have verified gym presence — QR check-in and check-out, plus busy-times. You have declared training intent — the /api/intent surface, where a member publishes what they are training today, auto-suggested from their active split and cycle position, with public / friends / private visibility and a feed of everyone else's. And you have a bounded social graph, because the gym defines the network.

Put together, Fitzo can answer a question no other product in this report can: who at my gym is training legs this evening, and are they there right now?

That is not a buddy-matching feature. Standalone buddy apps cannot build it because they have no verified presence and no bounded network — they are stuck acquiring both sides of a marketplace. Cult.fit has the gyms but no strength-training social layer. Hevy has the social layer but no gym. HealthifyMe has neither. Gym SaaS vendors have check-in data and no idea what to do with it.

Fitzo is the app your gym runs on: check in with it, know who is on the floor with you, train with them, and eat like someone who actually lives in India. Sell it to the gym; the gym hands you distribution and network density on day one.

Why B2B2C solves two problems at once

Selling to gyms is usually framed as a distribution choice. For Fitzo it is also the cold-start solution. The moment a gym adopts, its 300–800 members become a dense, co-located, real-identity network — the exact thing GymBudd and FitFriends burn capital trying to manufacture. Your social features are worthless at 5 users and excellent at 300 in one building.

This also reframes the gym-SaaS competition. You are not selling attendance software at ₹89 — you cannot win that. You are selling member retention, and you should price and pitch it that way: the gym's own churn problem, solved by an app members open daily because it holds their food, their split, and their friends.

Positioning against each competitor, in one line

  • vs HealthifyMe — "They give you a coach in an app. We give you your gym, your lifts, and the people on the floor with you."
  • vs Cult.fit — "For the gym you already go to."
  • vs Hevy — "Hevy knows your sets. Fitzo knows your sets, your macros, and who is at the rack next to you."
  • vs MyFitnessPal — "It knows what a Big Mac weighs. We know what a katori of dal weighs."
  • vs gym SaaS — "Their software runs your front desk. Ours makes members renew."
04 — Onboarding teardown

Your wizard is six screens long. That is the single most expensive thing in the product.

The benchmark, stated plainly

Health & fitness apps retain roughly 3–4% of installs at Day 30. The activation event that best predicts Day 30 retention is the first thing logged, not the install. Users who complete fewer than three sessions in their first fourteen days churn at 3–4× the rate of users who establish a weekly habit. The industry guidance is blunt: if someone installs a fitness app to log one workout and hits a six-screen wizard before they can log anything, they are gone.

Fitzo's OnboardingWizard is exactly that shape. TOTAL_STEPS = 6, and every one of them stands between install and first value.

What the six steps currently cost you

01
Body measurements — height, weight, age, gender
Needed for Mifflin-St Jeor. But only weight and a rough height are needed for a usable first estimate.
02
Goal — fat loss / muscle gain / maintain
Keep. One tap, and it changes everything downstream. This is the highest-information question you ask.
03
Activity level — sedentary through very active
Self-reported activity level is famously inaccurate. Default to moderate and correct it from real logged data within a week.
04
Nutrition blueprint — TDEE, macros, BMI reveal
This is a reward screen, not an input screen. It is currently gated behind three chores. Show it after the first log instead, where it lands as a payoff.
05
Training preference — split selection
A brand-new user does not know their split. Ask on the first workout, in context, where /intent/suggest can actually help.
06
Health sync — HealthKit / Google Fit permission
A permission prompt before any demonstrated value is the most-declined dialog in mobile. Ask on day three, after they have logged twice.

What competitors do instead

  • Cal AI — photograph a meal, see a result, then meet the paywall. Time to first value: seconds. This is what $30M ARR of onboarding looks like.
  • Hevy — start an empty workout immediately; profile and programme come later, if ever.
  • HealthifyMe — longer wizard, but they can afford it because the brand is pre-sold. You cannot.

The rebuild

Restructure around a single rule: nothing stands between install and the first logged item except things you cannot compute without.

  • Screen 1 — gym code. If B2B2C is the strategy, this is the first question, not an afterthought. Entering a gym code should immediately populate the squad feed, the class list and the busy-times chart. The app is never empty. This is your single biggest activation lever and it is currently nowhere in the wizard.
  • Screen 2 — goal + weight. Two fields. Enough for a defensible first calorie target.
  • Straight into a live log. Voice or photo, the thing you just rebuilt. Let them speak one meal and see it resolve against the Indian catalogue. That moment is the pitch.
  • Then the blueprint. Reveal TDEE, macros and BMI as the reward for logging, framed against what they just ate.
  • Defer the rest. Activity level, split, and health-sync permission become contextual prompts across the first week.

Target: two screens and under thirty seconds to first logged item, down from six screens. Instrument it — if you measure one funnel in this product, measure install → first log.
